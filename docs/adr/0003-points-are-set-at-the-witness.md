# Points are set at the Witness; approval records presentation

Until now a Submission's points were entered at approval: the approval panel took the base points and every bonus, and the member first learned their points when they were awarded. #498 (October 2026) moved the judgement to the Witness. The witness, who is looking at the fish, settles the Species, sets the base points (prefilled from the Point class) and every bonus but first-time, in the one transaction that confirms the Witness. Approval becomes the record that the member brought the fry, plants or coral to a meeting: it computes first-time, awards the total, and involves no judgement beyond the second tap.

## Considered options

- **Keep points at approval, show the member a projection earlier.** Rejected: the projection would be a second copy of the rule, and the committee member at the meeting still has to make the call with the fish long gone.
- **A proposal table, copied onto the Submission at approval.** Rejected: an extra table and a copy step for a value that already has columns. The existing columns hold the points from the Witness on; `approved_on IS NOT NULL` stays the one gate on totals, which every total-reporting query already filters on.
- **Set the points at the Witness, on the Submission's own columns.** Chosen.

## Consequences

- `confirmWitness` takes the Species, the base points and the bonuses, validated per Program by a Zod schema (CARES on BAP only, flowered and sexual reproduction on HAP only, base points 5/10/15/20), and writes them with the Witness in one transaction. It binds or rebinds to the Species the witness settled on and gives the Submission the Species' type, class and Program where they disagree, so a mismatch is no longer a refusal; the `MismatchError` is gone. The Program the bonuses are judged against is the Species' Program, the one the Submission is about to have, not the one the member filed under: the panel offers that Program's bonuses, the route's schema checks them against it, and the transition checks them again against the Species it resolves, so a HAP bonus never lands on a row that is becoming BAP.
- A Species with no Point class takes the chosen base points as its Point class, always. The witness assigns points for an unlisted Species on the spot; the manual's majority vote is not modelled.
- A Species the catalogue lacks is recorded on the witness panel (#522): names prefilled from the member's spelling, type and class from the Submission, the Point class and CARES flag chosen there. The Species is created inside the Witness's transaction, so a refused confirmation leaves no orphan and abandoning the panel creates nothing. The changelog's "Settled at the Witness" entry carries a `new_species` change, from which the approval queue derives "New species recorded by the witness"; nothing is stored on the Submission for it. The panel's "Did they mean one of these?" offers the catalogue's closest Species first, so a misspelt name does not become a duplicate.
- `first_time_species` stays NULL until approval, which computes it.
- A member's save that voids the Witness (ADR-0001) clears the points it set. The next witness starts from the catalogue's prefill.
- Points set at the Witness count toward nothing until approval. The invariants: an unapproved row with points is always witnessed; first-time is NULL on every unapproved row.
- Submissions witnessed under the old flow carry no points and are re-witnessed; there is no legacy approval path. Migration 066 (#524) sends every witnessed, unapproved, pointless row back to awaiting a Witness, clearing what a voided Witness clears and the dead `final_submission_on`, and leaves on each a plain-text changelog note attributed to the original witness: "Witness reset when points moved to the Witness; originally witnessed by X on D". The witness panel's header reads the note (`originalWitnessFromNotes`, src/lifecycle/rewitness.ts) and says so, since the fry were seen and only the Species and points are wanted; the clock counts from the reproduction date, so nothing restarts. A one-off post-deploy script (`scripts/email-rewitness.ts`, over `sendRewitnessEmails`) tells each affected member once that nothing is wrong, recording the send per member in `settings` so a second run emails nobody twice. The migration discards those old Witnesses (its rollback restores them to the day, from the notes), which is what makes this decision hard to reverse. After it, no witnessed, unapproved row lacks points.
- This supersedes ADR-0001's 2026-09-28 amendment: once the reset has run, no Submission is unbound past its Witness, because the Witness cannot be confirmed without settling the Species. After the Witness, the Species changes only together with the points, through a correction.
- The member learns the points when witnessed, while there is still time to dispute them, and sees them as "pending approval" until the meeting (#523): on their own Submission page and their own submissions list, itemised over the one Points rule (`pointsBreakdown`), and nowhere else. Public pages, other members' views of a profile and the standings show awarded points only, and a profile's totals sum awarded rows alone, so a witnessed row's columns never read as awarded.
- The member's "brought it to a meeting" step goes (#525). A witnessed Submission is in the approval queue the day its waiting period ends, with no member action; the states either side of that step merge under the name `inApprovalQueue`, the `enterApprovalQueue` and `removeFromQueue` moves and their routes are deleted, and `final_submission_on` becomes a dead column, read and written by nothing and never dropped (the denied_* precedent). Return to Draft is legal from the approval queue. The waiting-period email is a reminder to bring it to a meeting, sent once per Submission from the approval queue; the committee digest stops reporting the approval queue, since its rows wait on the member, not the committee.
